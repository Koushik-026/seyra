Five steps to an official seating plan

  1. Session: enter a name, date, start and end time for one exam sitting. Saving creates an empty sitting.
  2. Students: add students manually or preview and import CSV. The student master is reusable; only checked students in the saved session roster appear in this exam. Save your roster before continuing.
  3. Rooms: create reusable room layouts, then check “Use this room” for this sitting. Mark temporary unavailable benches on the room map and save the selection. Saving a layout alone does not select or reserve it.
  4. Review draft: generate from the saved roster and saved usable benches. Check the exam details, roster count, room maps, pairings and exclusions. A draft is private.
  5. Publish: explicitly confirm the reviewed version. It becomes available in student lookup. Print or export the selected version. Nothing is published automatically.

The next-action guide follows your saved progress. Unsaved selections must be saved before generation. Changing saved inputs makes affected drafts stale; generate again before publishing. The current publication keeps its original assignments while you prepare a replacement.

Room shape and bench numbers

Choose equal benches in every row for a regular room. Choose different counts by row for an uneven room: for example, Row 1 has 7 benches, Row 2 has 5 and Row 3 has 6. The plan contains exactly those 18 positions.

Rows run from the front to the back; bench numbers run left to right as shown in the preview. 1:2 means Row 1, Bench 2. 3:4 means Row 3, Bench 4. These are location labels, never ratios or room dimensions. Seats A and B are the two places on the same bench.

Click a bench to mark it unavailable. For a gap in the middle, keep that numbered position and mark it unavailable so later bench numbers stay consistent. Shorter rows use their actual count. A default exclusion belongs to the reusable layout; an extra session exclusion applies only to that sitting. The two sets combine without counting a bench twice.

Example: rows of 7, 5 and 6 give 18 bench positions. If two different benches are unavailable, 16 usable benches remain, with 32 usable seats. This is a numbered arrangement, not a scale drawing; aisles, distances and free-form geometry are not modeled.

Why spare seats can still be necessary

Two students may share a bench when their years differ OR their branches differ. Same year and branch cannot share, regardless of section. A student may sit alone.

For a non-empty roster, minimum benches = max(rounded-up student count ÷ 2, largest year-and-branch group). For 40 students with 30 in the same year/branch group, at least 30 benches are needed, even though 20 benches physically have 40 seats.

This formula assumes every student may use any selected room, exactly two seats per usable bench, no fixed seats, and exclusions of whole benches. Room shape does not change this calculation. The allocator and an independent validator check the actual usable positions.

Room reservations and times

Saved room selections reserve a whole room. Overlapping sessions cannot use it; back-to-back sessions are allowed. While preparing a replacement, both working rooms and currently published rooms stay protected. Removing a working room does not release it if the current plan still uses it.

Withdraw the current publication before changing session details. Withdrawal hides assignments; working selections remain reserved until you remove them. Historical plans retain their original room layouts.

Student lookup

Choose a published sitting and enter the full roll number, preserving leading zeros. The result shows the current room, row, bench, seat and version. A room map highlights your exact A or B seat, labels the front of the room and shows unavailable benches. Choose “View my seat in the room” to locate it. Other bench positions do not indicate occupancy. The map uses the published layout, even if the reusable room is later edited. A missing result can mean an incorrect roll, a different sitting or a withdrawn publication.
